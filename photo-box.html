PROJECT 01 · PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT

Automated Photo Box

A compact laboratory imaging system developed to automate multi-view product photography, weighing and data acquisition while improving repeatability and reducing manual handling.

01 / PROJECT & PROBLEM

Standardizing a slow, manual laboratory imaging workflow.

The existing process relied on manually positioning packaged products, photographing them in a separate imaging setup and entering product information into the laboratory workflow. The project objective was to combine these steps in a compact workstation-scale machine.

The engineering requirement was broader than simply placing cameras inside an enclosure: the system needed repeatable lighting, controlled backgrounds, multi-directional imaging, product weighing, reliable sensor integration and a layout that remained practical for daily use.

02 / SOLUTION

An integrated imaging, sensing and automation platform.

The final concept combines multiple camera views, controlled internal illumination, a transparent weighing platform, door-state sensing and onboard computing in one enclosure. The camera arrangement was developed around the available imaging volume so that relatively large packaged products could still be captured without making the machine impractically large for a laboratory bench.

The workflow was designed so that product loading, sensing and image acquisition could operate as one coordinated sequence rather than as separate manual tasks.

03 / ENGINEERING CHALLENGES

Mechanical, optical and mechatronic constraints had to be solved together.

01

Controlled lighting

The enclosure had to reproduce the consistency of a product-photography station. Diffusion, placement and enclosure geometry were developed to reduce unwanted shadows and reflections.

02

Compact camera packaging

The camera modules were moved toward the external corners of the enclosure to preserve usable imaging volume without increasing the overall footprint excessively.

03

Thermal & cable management

Cameras, computing hardware, lighting, fans and sensors created a dense internal layout. Component placement, service access, cooling paths and cable routing were iterated together.

04

Low-volume manufacturability

Early concepts used aluminium sheet and profile construction. Later iterations moved extensively to additive manufacturing to reduce weight, integrate complex geometry and avoid high tooling costs.

05

Sensing & machine logic

Weight and door-state sensing were integrated into the machine workflow through a custom interface board, enabling the mechanical system and automation sequence to operate coherently.

06

Prototype iteration

Mechanical and electronic design evolved through repeated build-test-adjust cycles until the architecture was suitable for a small series of functional units.

04 / TECHNOLOGY & SKILLS

Tools and engineering methods used throughout the development.

The project required a combination of mechanical design, prototyping, fabrication, electronics integration and practical validation rather than a single isolated discipline.
