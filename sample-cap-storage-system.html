PROJECT 05 · LAB EQUIPMENT · MECHANICAL PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT

Chemical-Resistant Sample Cap Storage System

A compact two-tray storage system developed inside an enclosed robotic laboratory workstation, allowing sample-container caps to remain organized, matched to their corresponding vessels and safely contained within the ventilated process environment.

01 / PROJECT & PROBLEM

Recovering lost process capacity inside a hazardous laboratory enclosure.

The laboratory system used a robotic arm to dispense liquids into sample containers inside a closed, extracted enclosure. Because the process could generate acidic and hazardous vapors, the enclosure needed to remain closed during operation.

Operators had to remove each sample cap before the automated process started. The caps themselves could carry traces of process liquid, so they also needed to remain inside the enclosure. Without a dedicated storage area, users placed the caps in the rear row of the sample rack. This prevented mix-ups, but effectively consumed a large portion of the machine's usable sample capacity.

02 / SOLUTION

Two sliding cap trays integrated into otherwise unused machine volume.

I identified the narrow space between the moving robotic system and the extraction area as the only practical unused volume. A dedicated structure was developed to carry two sliding trays, each with defined cap positions so operators could preserve the relationship between every cap and its corresponding sample container.

The trays remained entirely inside the ventilated enclosure, allowing the machine doors to stay closed while keeping contaminated caps within the controlled process environment.

03 / ENGINEERING CHALLENGES

Limited space, aggressive chemistry and moving equipment defined the design.

01

Restricted installation space

The structure had to fit into a narrow unused region while maintaining clearance from the robot throughout its motion envelope.

02

Chemical resistance

The enclosure atmosphere was corrosive, so conventional steel hardware could not be used. Structural connections and fasteners were selected around chemical-resistant non-metallic solutions.

03

Corrosion-resistant sliding motion

The tray rails required smooth movement without introducing vulnerable metallic bearings. Ceramic bearings were used for resistance to the acidic environment.

04

Cap identification

The tray geometry provided dedicated positions so sample caps could be stored in sequence and returned to the correct containers without mix-ups.

05

Robot clearance

The system had to remain outside the robot's working envelope in both tray positions while preserving operator access.

06

Retrofit integration

The solution was designed around an existing laboratory machine rather than a clean-sheet system, requiring careful packaging and installation planning.

04 / MY ROLE

Mechanical concept, packaging and detailed design.

I developed the mechanical concept, identified the available installation volume and designed the supporting structure, sliding trays, cap-position layout and chemically resistant motion system.

The solution was implemented for two laboratory machines and allowed the existing sample area to be used more effectively without moving contaminated caps outside the ventilated enclosure.
