PROJECT 04 · PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT · OPTICAL SYSTEM DESIGN

Automated Colony Imaging Station

A compact laboratory imaging enclosure developed to create repeatable, reflection-controlled photographs of Petri dishes for software-assisted colony monitoring and analysis.

01 / PROJECT & PROBLEM

Creating repeatable imaging conditions for biological samples.

Laboratory samples contained in Petri dishes needed to be photographed under controlled and repeatable conditions so software could monitor colony growth and support automated image analysis.

Although the product appears mechanically simple, reliable imaging depends on enclosure geometry, camera position, lighting, background, cooling and electronic control working together. The system also needed to remain compact, portable and suitable for routine benchtop use.

02 / SOLUTION

A compact 3D-printed enclosure with software-controlled illumination.

The product combined a camera module, controlled internal lighting, a dedicated Petri-dish imaging zone, thermal management and custom electronics inside a largely 3D-printed housing.

Two versions were developed: an earlier dark enclosure and a later green-and-white version intended for repeated laboratory deployment. The housing architecture was designed for straightforward assembly, service and reproduction of multiple units.

03 / ENGINEERING CHALLENGES

Optical consistency mattered more than mechanical complexity.

01

Reflection control

Lighting geometry had to minimize glare from the Petri dish and prevent unwanted reflections from contaminating the image.

02

Uniform illumination

The imaging area needed even, repeatable illumination so captured images remained suitable for downstream analysis.

03

Background optimization

The sample background was iterated to improve visual separation of colony features and support cleaner segmentation.

04

Camera cooling

The camera contained onboard processing hardware and generated heat, so a compact fan-based cooling solution was integrated.

05

Compact packaging

Camera hardware, lighting, cooling, wiring and electronics had to fit inside a small product without compromising serviceability.

06

Software-controlled lighting

The illumination system needed to operate automatically with the application software, requiring dedicated lighting-control electronics.

04 / MY ROLE

Mechanical product design and lighting-control electronics.

I was responsible for the mechanical design of the enclosure, internal packaging, cooling integration, assembly strategy and iterative development of the illumination geometry.

I also designed the electronic control board for the internal lighting so the software could trigger and manage the illumination automatically during image capture.
