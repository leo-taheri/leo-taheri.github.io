PROJECT 06 · STRUCTURAL DETAILING · SHEET-METAL ENGINEERING

Welded Steel Swimming Pool Structure

Shop-detailing and fabrication development for custom steel-framed swimming pools used in luxury residential projects, including full SolidWorks verification of structural members and welded steel sheet geometry before fabrication and installation.

01 / PROJECT & CONTEXT

Turning civil-engineering layouts into fabrication-ready pool structures.

The projects were custom steel swimming pools for high-end villas. Civil engineers supplied AutoCAD layouts, structural calculations and beam locations. My task was to convert those design documents into detailed, manufacturable shop drawings and complete 3D assemblies.

The pool structure used a steel frame that was subsequently clad with formed steel sheets. The sheet joints were welded to create the pool shell, making this type of construction suitable for locations where a conventional heavy concrete pool was not desirable, including elevated building levels.

02 / ENGINEERING WORKFLOW

Model first, verify digitally, then release fabrication drawings.

Structural members from the engineering drawings were recreated in SolidWorks and assembled into a complete pool frame. The steel lining was then developed over the structure as individual sheet-metal components so the complete pool shell could be checked virtually before anything was fabricated.

Each sheet was modelled in its final bent condition and then flattened to generate the cutting geometry. Before bending, the flat patterns were prepared for laser cutting from steel sheet. Every part received a unique identification code that could be marked on the cut component, allowing the bending workshop and installation team to match each physical sheet directly to its position on the assembly drawings.

After laser cutting, the sheets were bent one by one according to the fabrication drawings. The complete digital assembly made it possible to detect dimensional conflicts, incorrect interfaces and fit-up problems before the parts reached the site, significantly reducing rework during welding and installation.

03 / ENGINEERING CHALLENGES

Accuracy in sheet-metal geometry directly affected fabrication and on-site assembly.

01

Shop-drawing development

Engineering layouts had to be translated into production-ready structural and sheet-metal drawings with clear fabrication information.

02

Design-error detection

Rebuilding the project in 3D revealed dimensional inconsistencies and coordination problems before fabrication began.

03

Sheet-metal development

Pool surfaces had to be divided into practical steel panels, formed in CAD and converted into flat patterns for cutting and bending.

04

Welded joint coordination

Adjacent sheets had to meet correctly in the assembled geometry so workshop and site welding could be performed without forced fitting.

05

Fabrication feedback

Bending limitations, member cuts and actual workshop methods had to be reflected in the detailed design rather than treated as purely theoretical geometry.

06

Site installation

Installation supervision provided direct feedback on fit-up, bending accuracy, sheet positioning and practical assembly constraints.

04 / MY ROLE

3D structural detailing, sheet-metal development and installation support.

I was responsible for converting the supplied engineering layouts into detailed SolidWorks assemblies, modelling the structural frame, developing the steel sheet covering, preparing flat patterns and fabrication drawings, and checking the complete system digitally before production.

I also supported fabrication and supervised installation on site to verify that formed plates and structural components matched the intended geometry. Across the broader work program, I contributed to more than twenty pool structures.
