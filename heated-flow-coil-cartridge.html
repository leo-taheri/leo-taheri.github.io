PROJECT 09 · RAPID PROTOTYPING · CNC · ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING

Heated Flow Digestion Coil Cartridge Prototype

A cost-focused laboratory replacement prototype developed around a multi-meter flow tube embedded in a heated cartridge, progressing from a CNC-machined aluminum concept to a faster and cleaner additively manufactured polymer architecture.

01 / PROJECT & NEED

Recreating a costly heated flow-path cartridge for laboratory use.

The laboratory system used a long, fine tube routed through a heated cartridge. During operation, liquid flowed through the multi-meter tube while the cartridge was heated to approximately 100°C, providing a controlled heated residence path for the analytical process.

Because the original cartridge was expensive, the goal was to develop a practical alternative that reproduced the required tube length, fit the existing instrument and delivered comparable test results.

02 / FIRST APPROACH — CNC

Machining the flow path directly into aluminum plates.

The first concept used two aluminum plates. A long serpentine channel was machined into the plate with CNC milling, the tube was positioned inside the channel and the two halves were mechanically closed together.

This approach proved the geometry and gave a robust physical prototype, but the required fasteners projected from the assembly. That additional thickness created a packaging problem and prevented the cartridge from fitting cleanly inside the original instrument.

03 / SECOND APPROACH — 3D PRINTING

Moving the same functional idea to a printable polymer body.

The design was then reworked as two ABS parts produced by FDM 3D printing. The serpentine tube path was integrated directly into the printed geometry, allowing the long tube to be positioned quickly while eliminating the bulky external fastening arrangement of the machined version.

The printed solution was faster to manufacture, easier to iterate and cleaner in final assembly. An aluminum surface layer was added around the cartridge, and the completed prototype fit inside the original analyzer.

04 / VALIDATION

Functional testing matched the reference cartridge.

The brown cartridge shown in the project images is the original reference unit installed in the instrument. The aluminum-covered cartridge is the developed replacement prototype.

The redesigned cartridge was tested in the laboratory system and produced the same test result as the reference unit, demonstrating that the alternative manufacturing approach could reproduce the required function while improving manufacturability.

05 / MY ROLE

Concept development, CNC prototyping and additive-manufacturing redesign.

I developed both prototype routes, created the serpentine tube geometry, prepared the CNC-machined plate concept, redesigned the cartridge for FDM production, integrated the tubing and refined the package so it would fit the existing laboratory instrument.

The project was particularly useful as a comparison between subtractive and additive manufacturing: CNC machining was effective for the first proof of concept, while 3D printing provided the more practical final architecture.
